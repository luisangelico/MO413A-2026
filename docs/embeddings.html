What does an "embedding" represent here?

The short version. An embedding is the model's 64-number fingerprint of one sample. Two samples with similar embeddings are samples the model thinks look alike — based on both their gene expression and how that expression propagates through the protein–protein interaction network.

How it's built. The model processes each sample's PPI graph through three GAT layers. Each of the 1000 genes ends up with a 64-number vector that mixes its own expression with attention-weighted information from its PPI neighbors (and their neighbors, and theirs — three hops out). The model then averages those 1000 vectors into one 64-dimensional summary of the whole sample. That averaged vector is the embedding.

What it represents. Think of it as the model's compressed answer to: "if I had to describe this sample with 64 numbers, capturing both what genes are highly expressed AND how those genes relate to each other through known protein interactions, what would they be?"

Two samples will have similar embeddings if they share expression patterns in the genes the model learned matter, and the attention flow over the PPI graph reaches the same conclusion about them. That's stronger than "similar expression" alone — what matters is the expression plus how it propagates through the network.

What it is not.

  • Not the prediction. The prediction is one more step: a linear layer turns the 64-d embedding into 3 class logits. The embedding lives upstream of that decision.
  • Not interpretable per-dimension. Each of the 64 numbers has no individual meaning — only the geometry (distances, directions, neighborhoods) is meaningful.
  • Not biological coordinates. There's no axis labeled "tumor-ness" or "immune activity" — those are things we find by analysis (see the pseudotime page), not things the model was told to encode.

Why we look at them. The embedding is the most compact, lossy-but-faithful fingerprint of the sample as the model sees it. The geometry below — clusters, neighborhoods, distances — is the model's view of how skin samples relate to each other, with the noise in the original 1000-gene vector compressed out by training.
